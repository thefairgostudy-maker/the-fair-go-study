THE BIG QUESTION
Does everyone have a fair chance to succeed?

The study asks young Australians what they think — and what they believe shapes the opportunities people have.

The idea

Australia is built around the idea of a “fair go”.

But what does that actually mean to young Australians?

The Fair Go Study asks students across Australia to share their views on success, opportunity and fairness.

Does working hard give everyone the same chance to succeed? How much do family circumstances, education or where someone lives matter? And do young people think Australia lives up to the idea of a fair go?

Rather than assuming the answers, the study asks students directly.

The research

What are we asking?

The study looks at how young Australians understand the things that can shape a person's chances in life.

What does success mean?
Is success mainly about hard work, or do other things shape where someone ends up?
What shapes opportunity?
How much can family, education, location and circumstances affect the chances people have?
What is a “fair go”?
How do young Australians understand fairness and equal opportunity?
Does Australia provide one?
Do students believe everyone has a genuine chance to succeed?
From research to impact

What happens to the answers?

The survey is only the beginning. The findings are intended to become a resource that can contribute to wider conversations about opportunity, inequality and policy.

After 23 October 2026

The survey will close and the responses will be analysed. The findings will be brought together in the Fair Go Report.

The report will be published and the findings can be shared with MPs, ministers, government departments and organisations working with young people.

The goal: make young people's perspectives part of the conversation about the policies and systems that shape their opportunities.
